# Content Writing

## When to use
Use whenever text is needed for the storefront or customer communications:
product descriptions, category pages, policies (shipping/returns/contact),
and transactional emails (order confirmation, shipping, receipts).

## Core rules
- Tone: trustworthy, professional, plain language. Short sentences, active
  voice, no hype or exaggeration; this is construction equipment — buyers
  want facts and reliability.
- Product description structure:
  1. Benefit-led opener (1-2 sentences: what the tool does for the buyer,
     e.g., "Drives nails faster on site").
  2. Specs table (power, size, weight, voltage, capacity, warranty).
  3. Usage/ideal-fit line ("For: contractors, framing, weekend repairs").
  4. What's in the box + safety note if relevant.
- Bullet specs over prose for comparison; consistent units (metric + imperial
  where relevant); always state warranty and certifications.
- Category descriptions: 1-2 paragraphs, define who it's for and what they
  can find; internal links to top products.
- Store policies: shipping (times, costs, areas), returns (window, conditions,
  process), contact — write them to reduce support tickets; be specific,
  never vague ("usually 3-5 days" -> "3-5 business days").
- Transactional emails: short, scannable; clear subject line ("Order
  #1023 confirmed — arriving Mon"); one job each: confirm, ship, deliver,
  receipt; include order summary, tracking link, support contact, and a
  single soft CTA (reorder/review) — never a hard-sell.
- SEO-aware: weave the primary keyword into title/H1/first paragraph
  naturally; never keyword-stuff; keywords must match actual product naming
  people search for ("power drill" vs "rotary impact drill").
- Consistency: keep a shared glossary (product names, units, terms) so
  listings match the catalog data.

## Do NOT
- Do not copy manufacturer boilerplate verbatim or invent specs.
- Do not use marketing superlatives ("best ever", "ultimate").
- Do not bury prices or conditions in fine print.
- Do not write emails that read like newsletters — transactional only.
